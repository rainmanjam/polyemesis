# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are acting in good faith. Criticise ideas and
code, not the person who wrote them.

## What is expected

- **Be direct and be kind.** Those are not in tension. "This will drop frames on
  a slow disk, here's why" is both.
- **Assume competence.** Someone asking a basic question may be an expert in
  something you are not. Someone whose patch has a bug is not careless.
- **Stay on the technical question.** Disagreements about design are the point of
  a project like this. Disagreements about a person are not.
- **Accept that maintainers say no.** This project has explicit constraints (see
  [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)). A declined change is not a judgement of
  you.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments relating to age, disability, ethnicity,
  gender identity or expression, nationality, personal appearance, race,
  religion, or sexual orientation.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing others' private information without permission.
- Sustained disruption — of issues, pull requests, or discussion.

## Scope

This applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, and any place someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to the maintainer at **rainmanjam@gmail.com**. Reports are
handled privately.

The response will be proportionate. Most problems are a bad day or a
misunderstanding and end with a private word. Where more is needed the
maintainer may edit or remove content, ask someone to step away from a thread,
or block an account — the last of these for repeated or serious behaviour.

If you are the subject of a report you will be told what was reported and given
a chance to respond, unless doing so would put the reporter at risk.
